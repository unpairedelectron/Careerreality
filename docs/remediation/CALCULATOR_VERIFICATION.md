## Rules represented by the salary-only estimate

- Default new-regime slabs for Tax Year 2026–27: ₹0–4 lakh nil; ₹4–8 lakh 5%; ₹8–12 lakh 10%; ₹12–16 lakh 15%; ₹16–20 lakh 20%; ₹20–24 lakh 25%; above ₹24 lakh 30%.
- The Section 202(4) old-regime option uses the applicable First Schedule bands: ₹0–2.5 lakh nil; ₹2.5–5 lakh 5%; ₹5–10 lakh 20%; above ₹10 lakh 30%.
- Standard deduction is ₹75,000 for the new regime and ₹50,000 for the old regime. The old-regime estimate combines eligible employee PF and the entered old-regime deduction amount under a ₹1.5 lakh cap.
- Professional tax is entered manually and deducted as an employment-tax deduction; no state/UT rate is inferred.
- The new-regime rebate is available up to ₹12 lakh total income, capped at ₹60,000; the statutory marginal-relief rule applies immediately above the threshold. The old-regime rebate is available up to ₹5 lakh, capped at ₹12,500; the model does not invent a marginal-relief rule above that threshold.
- Surcharge rate bands and marginal relief follow the official text. For income chargeable under Section 202, the Finance Act 2026 memorandum limits surcharge above ₹5 crore to 25% in both regimes; do not apply the older 37% rate to this estimate.
- Health and Education Cess is 4% on income tax inclusive of surcharge. The model does not grant marginal relief against cess.

This is deliberately narrower than a tax return calculation: resident salaried individual below age 60; salary-only; no special-rate income, exemptions, equity valuation, reimbursements, arrears, other income, or filing-specific adjustments.

## PF transition model and limits

The model uses a monthly basic-wage estimate and the user-entered PF employment start date. April–August 2026 use the ₹15,000 monthly ceiling; September is split into 1–16 September at ₹15,000 and 17–30 September at ₹25,000 (16/14 days); October 2026–March 2027 use ₹25,000. EPFO FAQ questions 7 and 9 show September's separate periods and proportional wages. Partial start months are prorated by calendar days. The model does not decide EPF/EPS membership, eligibility, employer-specific wage treatment, deferred employee recovery, or other case-specific rules, and the start date affects PF only—not annual tax or gross-pay estimates.
